# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to each other. Assume the person on the other end is acting
in good faith, is busy, and may not share your first language.

## What that means here

**Expected:** focus criticism on code and ideas; accept that
disagreement is normal and that the maintainer may decline a change
without it being a judgement on you; be patient with questions that
seem obvious to you.

**Not accepted:** personal attacks, harassment of any kind, demeaning
comments about someone's identity or background, deliberate derailing,
sustained pressure after a decision has been made, or publishing
someone's private information.

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, and any other
project space.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately through GitHub — use the repository's
private vulnerability reporting form, which is the only private channel
this project has, and label the report clearly as a conduct issue
rather than a security one.

Reports are read by the maintainer alone. If a report concerns the
maintainer, you are unlikely to get a satisfying resolution from an
internal process, and GitHub's own [reporting
tools](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github)
are the better path. Saying so plainly is more useful than implying an
impartiality that a one-person project does not have.

## Consequences

Depending on severity: a warning, deletion of the offending comment,
or a block from the repository. The maintainer decides, and the
decision is final. There is no appeals body — again, this is a
one-person project, and pretending otherwise would be theatre.

## Scope

This applies to project spaces and to situations where someone is
representing the project in public. It is not a general licence to
police contributors' unrelated conduct elsewhere.
